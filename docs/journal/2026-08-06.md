# Journal - 2026-08-06

A long session, and the shape of it is worth writing down because it is the
shape the whole project keeps taking: the parts were fine, the checks were the
problem, and every "bug" was one layer out from where it first looked.

## Where it started

Picked up the branch at four servos floating half a millimetre off the parts
they bolt to - held by nothing, and green on every connectivity check because
each one still hung on the assembly through its own horn. Gave them seating
faces. The hip took a clean end wall. The roll servo could not: any seat on the
shaft axis lives inside the servo's own interference envelope, so it got a
single datum wall instead. Straightforward, or it looked it - the hip seat I
added came back as a non-manifold chassis STL, four open edges, the kind a print
service rejects before it quotes. Caught only by printability, because a
zero-width crack weighs nothing and interferes with nothing. Ran the wall out to
share a face instead of meeting along an edge. Fixed.

## The robot was weighed in the wrong plastic

Then the mass. The printed structure had been quoted at PETG's 1.270 g/cm3
because that is what it was costed in - but it is ordered in PA6-CF at 1.19. The
density was copied across five files. Pulled it into one, cad/material.py, and
the robot came out 22 g lighter: 1748.9 g, and the balancer still stands and
rejects a shove at the new mass. One number, five places, the repo's oldest
rule broken five ways.

## Two open items that were never real

The "shin reaches y=136" item turned out to be a ghost - the belt that pushed
the bearing out there was deleted days ago, the bearing came home to y=71, and
only the comment stayed behind. Struck it. The "FEA is optimistic about infill"
item was backwards: the allowable is already knocked 0.60. What was wrong was a
comment claiming stiffness was knocked too; it was not. Fixed the comment, not
the code, and did NOT nearly-double every deflection into a number the parts do
not have.

## The rod, finally solved

cad.stress had never touched a linkage rod. Turned out it should not - the rod
is buckling-limited, and a linear-static solver cannot see buckling. But the
tension case, the pin eye, nobody had solved. Pointed cad.fea at it: 2% of
allowable, buckling 20x. And the buckling test had frozen a stale torque while
its own docstring said "measured, not a literal." Made it live.

## The fasteners, and the day's real lesson

Checked whether the CAD holes matched the bolt list. They did not. Sixteen
heat-set inserts on the order sheet with no 3.5 mm bore anywhere in the
geometry - a dead constant and a clearance hole where an insert wanted a bore.
Wrote the check that catches it, then cut the bores: the Pi standoffs, and -
after dropping eight inserts for a chassis joint that does not exist on a
one-piece print - two set screws per yoke to retain the ankle-pitch shaft.

That last one is where the day earned its length. Placing the yoke inserts
turned the suite red on two FEA tests. The bore was the obvious suspect. I built
a whole mechanism to route the global mesh around it - and the routed-around
version failed identically. The bore was innocent. The whole-leg FEA had been
reading a STALE step file off disk, passing on old geometry while the parts
moved underneath it; regenerating it exposed that the mesher emits a single
orphan node, and one node with no stiffness makes the entire matrix singular.
Pinned orphan nodes in the solver, reverted the wrong fix, and the leg converged
again. Full suite: 109 passed.

## What I keep relearning here

The thing you changed last is the loudest suspect and usually the wrong one. The
control - does it still break without my change - is what points at the truth.
And a green check is not evidence; it is a claim, and half of them today were
reading a copy, a stale file, or the wrong plastic.

## Where it leaves us

Everything the digital twin can settle on its own is settled and green. The two
open items - real PA6-CF strength, gear backlash - both need a physical part, and
that is the $30 validation order, not more simulation. The model has, as far as
its own checks can prove, converged.
