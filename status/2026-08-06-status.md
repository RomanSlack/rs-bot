# 2026-08-06 - two open items were measured, and neither was real

## What turned out to be wrong

The bugs today were in the OPEN-ITEMS LIST, not the model. Two things carried on
it as work-to-do were checked against the geometry for the first time and struck.
Both had been believed because they were written down, which is this repo's
oldest failure (how-checks-fail.md #9), and this time the loop itself was the one
repeating them: each rode a status report forward without anyone measuring.

## The shin does not reach y = 136

The list said "the shin's forward carrier reaches y = 136; bringing it in moves
the ankle-pitch axis, a kinematics decision." Built and measured, the shin runs
**y = 15..76**. The ankle-pitch bearing is at PITCH_Y = 71, where cad/envelope.py
forces it - the only outboard band is 56..80, inboard collides with the wheel or
pairs the two bearings 6 mm apart. There is no y = 136 member to bring in.

The 136 was belt-era. The ankle-pitch belt once shoved the bore ~53 mm off axis;
the belt was deleted 2026-08-03 and the bore came back on axis, but the comment
in cad/shin.py did not follow, and from there it copied into the 08-04 and 08-05
lists. Corrected at the source, in the comment that started it.

## The FEA does not overstate strength - it was already knocking infill down

The list said the FEA "runs on solid material while parts print at 60% infill, so
the strength may be optimistic the way the mass was heavy." It is not. cad/stress
already multiplies the ALLOWABLE by 0.60 for every FDM material (`knockdown()`),
so a part fails at 60% of its coupon ultimate. That is the conservative
direction, and the shin's PA6-CF utilisation is 66% in-plane / 67% interlayer
against a knocked allowable, not against solid.

What WAS wrong was one line of the comment, and it is the kind worth catching:
the header claimed the 0.60 knocked "both strength and stiffness". Only strength
was. Deflection was solved at solid E - correctly, because bending stiffness
lives in the solid perimeters at the extreme fibre, so solid E is close to real
and a lower bound, the same footing docs/assembled-strength.md already uses. The
comment made a reader believe the deflections were conservative when they are a
lower bound. Had someone "fixed the code to match the comment" - which was the
first thing tried here - every FDM deflection would have nearly doubled (the shin
from 6.8 to 11.3 mm) into a number the part does not have. Fixed the comment, not
the code: net behaviour unchanged, no deflection number moves.

One inert straggler noted, not fixed: cad/stress carries PA6-CF at 1.15 g/cm3
while cad/material.py now says 1.19. It is a different value for one material in
two files (#5), but `rho` is set and never read in the stress pipeline (#6), so
it is dead. Resolving which datasheet is right is a coupon question, not a
guess to make today.

## And the linkage rod finally got solved - and a test caught up to the mass

"cad.stress has never solved a linkage rod" had sat on the list for a fortnight,
and it turned out to be half a real gap and half a category error. A two-force
member fails two ways, and only one of them belongs in cad.stress at all:

- COMPRESSION is buckling, which a linear-static field solver cannot see. That
  is why the rod never went into cad.stress, and it was right not to. Euler in
  cad/linkage.loads() has always had it, at **20.7x** on the live load.
- TENSION pulls on the pin EYE, whose stress concentration the net-section hand
  calc in the comment could not give. THAT is the number nothing had solved.
  cad/linkage.rod_stress() points cad.fea at the rod for the first time and gets
  **1.2 MPa, 2% of the knocked PA6-CF allowable**, mesh-converged at 1.3 and
  1.6 mm. So "sized by buckling, not stress" is a solver result now, and both
  modes have a test: test_the_rod_eye_is_far_below_allowable and
  test_the_rods_are_nowhere_near_buckling.

Solving it surfaced the actual bug, which was in the test, not the rod. The
buckling test's docstring said "sized on the MEASURED torque, not a literal"
while the code passed `torque_nm=1.082` - a literal, and a stale one: the live
survey reads **0.95 N.m** since the robot dropped to PA6-CF, so the frozen force
was 14% high (how-checks-fail #20, the very trap the docstring named). It now
calls the live survey, which is 10 s and not the "couple of minutes" its
comment feared, and asserts the buckling MARGIN rather than a copied force that
went stale on every mass change. The stale 38.7 N / 18x in cad/linkage.py's own
comments were corrected to the live 34 N / 20.7x.

## And the fasteners were a paper claim the geometry never backed

Checking whether the CAD holes match the bolt list - the kind of paper-vs-solid
link this project exists to police - turned up the sharpest gap of the day. The
BOM orders **16 M2.5 heat-set inserts**, and there is not one **3.5 mm bore in
any part to seat them**: `M25_INSERT_R` was defined and used nowhere (a dead
constant, #6), and where the schedule says "insert in chassis" the chassis cuts
a 2.7 mm clearance hole straight through. Every part-to-part joint - chassis
assembly, Pi mount, ankle-pitch retention - has no thread. Print today and it
stops at assembly, a fortnight gone. The `order-sheet.md` fastener table had
drifted the other way too: 40 M2 case screws deleted with the capture cradles,
and missing the 8 M3x6 horn centre screws and 4 M3x12 + 4 nuts the rod clamp
needs, while claiming to be "generated by cad.fasteners".

Nothing caught it because `audit()` checks servo-screw ALIGNMENT (the horn
holes, which exist) and `bom()` counts inserts off the schedule - intent, never
geometry. Two checks now do: `fasteners.inserts_seated()` asks the solid for a
bore per insert and reports the shortfall (chassis 12, yoke 2), and
`fastener_order()` plus a test holds the order sheet equal to the schedule.
Both control-tested - the insert check detects a real blind bore and ignores a
clearance hole - and the shortfall was PINNED in tests/test_fasteners.py the way
the floating servos were.

**Then the bores went in and the interface closed.** Three ways: the 4 Pi
standoff bores were cut (an insert in the standoff top, not the 2.7 mm
through-hole that matched no fastener); the 8 "side plates -> top" inserts were
DROPPED, a joint a one-piece fused chassis does not have, taking the order from
16 inserts to 8; the 4 yoke inserts became two radial M2.5 set screws per pair,
retaining the ankle-pitch shaft from the boss's exposed +x face. `inserts_seated`
reads empty, the pin flipped to `== []`, and it verified clean - interference
through the flip, meshes manifold, sim = cad at 1748.9 g.

Placing them extended `toolaccess`: it had flagged the insert bores as
unreachable because it treated them as driver-driven screws. A heat-set insert
is a bench operation - pressed with an iron before assembly, its screw arriving
from the mating part - exactly like the horn screws it already excused. It now
excludes them, its control test still catches a real slab across a driver, and
what the exclusion does NOT cover (the yoke set screws, driven in the assembled
robot with a hex key) is named in the check itself.

## And placing the yoke inserts uncovered a stale FEA the suite had never noticed

The two yoke set-screw bores went in - radial from the boss's exposed +x face
onto the pitch shaft - and `inserts_seated` reached empty. But the full suite
then failed two stress tests, and chasing it was the real work of the day.

The whole-leg FEA (`leg_assembly`) reads its STEP off disk and nothing
regenerates it, so it had been solving a STALE fused leg and passing (#23).
Regenerating it to current geometry, the solve went singular. The bore was the
loudest suspect and the wrong one: a "defeature the yoke for the global mesh"
workaround, built first, failed identically. The real cause was ONE orphan node
the mesher emits, zero-stiffness, enough to make the matrix singular.
`cad/fea.solve` now pins orphan nodes (a disconnected CHUNK still trips the guard
that matters), the defeature was reverted, and the leg converges at 36 k nodes.
The net of the whole saga is small and right: the inserts plus a one-block fix
in the solver.

simulation. The digital twin has, as far as its own checks can prove, converged.

Item 1 is the last thing the model can settle on its own - I said yesterday
