# 2026-08-04 - four servos were held by nothing, and no check could see it

## What turned out to be wrong

Someone looked at the assembly viewer and said parts were still floating. Every
check was green. They were right and the checks were not wrong either, which is
the whole content of this report.

**Four of the six servos touch no part of the robot.** The two hip servos sit
0.400 mm off the chassis, the two roll servos 0.400 mm off the ankle yoke. Not
in a cradle bolted through a face - off it, with air on every side.

```
   vkneesv_l -> thigh_l          0.000 mm     seated
   vwhlsv_l  -> rollbracket_l    0.000 mm     seated
   vhipsv1   -> torso            0.400 mm     held by nothing
   vrollsv_l -> ankle_l          0.400 mm     held by nothing
```

0.400 is `CRADLE_CLEAR`. A cradle is cut 0.4 mm oversize on purpose so the
servo drops in, and that part is correct. What is missing is a seating FACE:
two of the four mounts have one and two do not, and a cradle you can drop a
servo into is not a cradle that locates it.

## Why nothing caught it

Three checks ask "is the robot one connected object" and all three were right
to say yes. `fitcheck.connectivity()` at 4 mm, `assemble_check.connected()` at
0.05 mm on real solids, and two tests asserting both. A floating servo is still
joined to the assembly **through its own output horn**, so the graph stays
connected. The graph was never the problem.

The problem is that a servo and the part it seats against are the SAME RIGID
BODY. They move as one lump and have nothing between them. Union-find over the
whole robot at once cannot ask that, because it is a local question and the
answer it gives is one global number.

There is a second reason it stayed hidden, and it is worse: **`cad.fasteners`
has been reporting this for weeks in different units.** "Worst is 1.30 deg" of
servo capture preload IS this 0.4 mm, measured from the other end. Two checks
describing one fault in units that do not obviously match is its own way of
being invisible.

## The tool

`cad/floating.py`, and it asks the two local questions instead:

- **a rigid body has to be one piece** - union-find WITHIN a body, at a seam
  rather than at a running clearance. `loose()`.
- **a connector has to reach both ends** - a shaft touching one body drives
  nothing. `unreached()`.

They need to be separate because the tells are opposite. A bracket that touches
one body is fine; a shaft that touches one body is not.

`cad/fitview.py` paints whatever it reports **red**, with a "held by nothing"
panel at the top and click-to-isolate onto the part and the face it should be
meeting. That is the half that matters: a list of names in a terminal makes you
hunt for the part, and the reason these survived is that nobody could see them.

**Two traps in writing it, both of which produced a confident wrong answer.**

- Reach has to be TRANSITIVE. The first version reported all four ankle shafts
  as reaching nothing, because a shaft does not touch the parts it joins, it
  touches the BEARING pressed into them. Four false alarms is how you teach
  somebody to ignore a check.
- The control test has to push in a direction that actually separates. Nudging
  the knee servo 2 mm into its cradle left the gap at 0.000, because the thigh
  wraps it on three sides; the check looked broken and was not. `+y` gives
  0.085 mm and is reported. `tests/test_floating.py` pins the working nudge and
  says why.

Runtime is 1.7 s after a bounding-box prefilter, so it runs inside the viewer
rather than being a thing you remember to do.

## And the shell for the servo that is not there

The ankle-pitch servo was deleted on 2026-08-03. Its mount outlived it by a
day: `STANDOFF`, two cradle side walls, an upper end wall and a clearance
pocket, all holding a part that is not on the order sheet.

**53.05 cm3 to 45.11**, about 9 g a leg, off the part with the least mass left
to give. The robot is **1769.8 g**, sim and CAD agreeing.

`cad.twin` caught the consequences immediately and in the right direction:
`vankstand_l` went to 0.0% backed, NOT IN THE CAD, and the shin read 8.8 g
heavy until `SEG_INERTIA` was regenerated.

Three things fell out of it:

- `cad/assembly.py` was still drawing an ankle-pitch servo, placed off
  `shin.STANDOFF`. The last viewer showing a robot that does not exist.
- The shin can mesh at 2.2 mm again, 43 680 nodes. It had been bracketed at
  2.25 yesterday because the knee stub pushed it past `fea.MAX_NODES`; that is
  not a mesh fix, the part is simply 8 cm3 smaller.
- `docs/mass-budget.md`'s parallelogram row said 2 x 35 g and the table did not
  sum to its own total. 35 g a leg was right while the fin, stub and arm were
  loose solids; they are host features now, so the rods and idler alone are
  17 g. The order sheet still said **1.907 kg**, two changes behind.

## What is still open, in rank

1. **The four floating servos.** A seating face on the chassis and the ankle
   yoke, the way the thigh and the roll bracket already have one. This is the
   1.3 deg capture preload on the old list, now with a number in millimetres
   and a check that fails until it is fixed.
2. The shin's forward carrier - FARM, FPOST, CROSS, DROP, BACK - reaches
   y = 136 because the deleted belt forced the ankle-pitch bearing out there.
   Bringing it in MOVES THE ANKLE PITCH AXIS, so it is a kinematics decision
   rather than a tidy-up. Flagged, not taken.
3. Printed mass quoted at PETG's density for parts ordered in PA6-CF, ~26 g.
4. Backlash unmeasured on real hardware, which everything above is quoted
   against.
5. `cad.stress` has never solved a linkage rod.
