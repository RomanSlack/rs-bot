# 2026-08-05 - the four floating servos get a face, and one seat cannot be where you want it

## What turned out to be wrong

Nothing new. This is the top item off 2026-08-04's list, taken and closed: the
four servos that were held by nothing now bear on a seating face. The two hip
servos sat 0.400 mm off the chassis and the two roll servos 0.400 mm off the
ankle yoke - `CRADLE_CLEAR` on every wall, so the cradle you drop a servo into
located it on no axis at all, and the torso's load into the legs ran through two
M3 screws in air.

`cad/floating.py` found it, and it is now green. Two independent checks that
describe the same fault from other ends moved with it, which is the confirmation
that matters more than floating going quiet:

- `cad.fasteners.capture()` went from **1.30 deg to 0.20 deg** at the hips and
  **0.10 deg** at the roll cradles. That 1.3 deg WAS the 0.4 mm, measured as
  free rotation about the shaft; closing the gap closes the angle.
- `cad.assemble_check` reports the servo cases seated, not floating.

The robot is **1771.0 g**, sim and CAD agreeing. Up 1 g, the only addition in
the run.

## The fix, and why the two ends are not the same fix

The good mounts already in the repo - the thigh's knee servo, the roll bracket's
wheel servo - seat on a face PERPENDICULAR to the cradle walls, flush, so the
case bottoms on it. A flush WALL cannot do this job: with print tolerance at
+/-0.3 mm a wall drawn to the case size can come out 0.3 mm small and the servo
will not go in, which is the whole reason `CRADLE_CLEAR` is 0.4 and exceeds it.
The seat has to be a face the servo meets along its INSERTION direction, not one
it is pressed between.

**The hip (chassis) took the clean version.** The servo's lower z face already
had 2.5 mm of side plate reaching past it (`SIDE_Z0 = HIP_SERVO_Z[0] -
CRADLE_WALL`), so an end wall flush at `HIP_SERVO_Z[0]` seats the case and sits
OUTSIDE the servo's interference envelope, below it. `assemble_check` passed it
without comment.

**The roll (ankle) could not.** The first attempt reached the "servo face" ring
in to the case body output face at x = -61.4 - correcting a real latent bug,
because the ring had been built to the envelope's spline TIP at -58 and stood
3.4 mm off the servo seating nothing. It seated the case at 0.000 on the real
mesh, and `floating` and `fitcheck` were happy. Then `assemble_check` reported
**828 mm3 of overlap** with the roll servo, constant across the flip, and it was
right: the servo's interference BOX is solid from -94.5 to -58, so any seat that
touches the case output face on the shaft axis is buried inside the box on one
side or the other. The spline is round and clears in reality, but the envelope
reserves that corner, and a seat is not allowed to spend reserved space on the
strength of "the real part is thinner here". That is how-checks-fail #12 in a
new place: the mesh checks and the box check disagree on a shaft-axis seat, and
the box is the one to believe.

So the roll seat is the other legal option: **one wall is the datum**. The +y
wall goes flush to the case, the -y wall keeps `CRADLE_CLEAR`. The servo slides
in along its shaft (x), not down between the walls, so a flush wall is not a
press fit - it just gives the case a face to register against, and the opposite
wall still lets it drop in. The bottom face, which would have been the
thigh-style perpendicular seat, is the one the foot-mode floor rules out, and
the yoke already carries that note.

## The hip seat left the chassis STL non-manifold, and only one check saw it

The hip end wall was drawn to +/-xi, which is where the two cradle walls START.
That met each wall along a single EDGE at z = HIP_SERVO_Z[0] and nowhere else,
and an edge-only join is not a surface a printer can slice: the export came out
with four open edges, the exact thing a print service's own checker rejects
before it quotes. `cad/printability.py` was the only check that caught it.
`floating`, `assemble_check`, `fitcheck` and `twin` were all green on the same
geometry, because a crack that is zero millimetres wide interferes with nothing,
weighs nothing and stays connected. The fix runs the wall out to +/-xo so it
shares a FACE with each cradle wall and the outer x face runs continuous, the
way those walls already meet the side plate.

Two things this is worth remembering for. First, a seating face added against
existing structure has to OVERLAP it or share a full face - abut it along an
edge and the solid is still valid in every geometric check but the mesh is not.
Second, this was caught only because the loop says re-derive the state rather
than trust the last report: yesterday's run never called `printability`, so the
regression would have sat green in the summary while the one file that has to be
correct on order day pointed at an STL no service would take.

## The robot was weighed in the wrong plastic, in five files

The printed structure was quoted at PETG's 1.270 g/cm3. It is ordered in PA6-CF
at 1.19 - `docs/materials.md` settled that months ago, "the only material that
passes" - and the only reason the mass used PETG was that PETG "is what those
parts were costed in". Costed in is not ordered in. Weighing a part off its cost
basis made the whole robot read **22 g heavy**: 1771 g against a real 1749.

The number was in five files - `cad/chassis`, `cad/ankle`, `cad/thigh`,
`cad/shin` and `cad/masses` - all reading 1.270, which is the repo's own rule
broken five ways and four places waiting to drift. It is now in `cad/material.py`
alone, a leaf module the CAD parts and the physics mass table both import, the
way `servo_dims` and `wheel_dims` already hold the numbers that used to be
copied. `cad.twin` confirms sim = CAD at every body and 1749.0 g total.

The mass feeds the balancer, so it was re-checked and not assumed: all 14 of
`tests/test_stage0.py` pass at the new mass - stands 60 s unattended, rejects
0.35 and 1.0 N.s shoves both directions, tracks 0.25 m/s, steers. The margin the
lighter robot spends is already inside the thresholds, which were themselves
re-measured the last time it lost mass rather than relaxed.
