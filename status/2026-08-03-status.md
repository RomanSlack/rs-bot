**2026-08-03** · 87 tests green · **handoff.** The one part that blocked an
order went from 99% to 55% for 1.5 mm of web, the bought hardware that actually
holds this robot together got modelled for the first time, and a passive

## What is true right now

Every check passes. `uv run python -m pytest tests -q` is **87 passed**, about
22 minutes. `drives`, `twin` (all three of its checks), `assemble_check` (all
four), `fitcheck`, `wiring`, `toolaccess`, `printability`, `fasteners` and
`servo` are all clean, and so is `linkage`, which is new.

Two servers, both useful:

```
uv run python serve.py          :8781  drive it, flip it (F), shove it (space)

```

`cad.fitview` takes about four minutes to come up per mode because it exports 53
meshes and measures every pairwise gap. That is a wart, not a hang.

## What changed since the 08-02 report

### The roll bracket stopped blocking the order

It was at 99% of PA6-CF, converged, and over in all three PA12 variants. It is
at **55%**, and passes in all four.

```
web 4.0 mm   56.6 MPa   99% PA6-CF   118/126/135% in the PA12s
web 5.5 mm   31.4 MPa   55% PA6-CF    66/ 70/ 75%
```

Stress in a bending plate goes as 1/thickness, so the last 1.5 mm was worth 44
points. Hub radius was tried first and is not the lever - 8 to 12 mm moves it
99% to 97%, so it was never a concentration, just too little section. The 1.5 mm
came out of the yoke's post, which was 6.0 mm housing a 4.0 mm bearing.

**Three separate yoke boxes referenced the old web face and only one moved with
it.** `assemble_check` found the other two at 477 mm3 and 45 mm3, and `twin`
found the sim's post box still describing the 6 mm part at 37.9% backed. None of
that was visible in the diff.

### The bought hardware exists now

`cad/hardware.py` is new. Bearings, shafts, pulleys, the belt and the ten horns
were on the order sheet and drawn nowhere, so the shin and the ankle yoke had
literally nothing between them and nothing could ask whether a shaft was long
enough for its bore. They are in the CAD and in the sim, and the belt's 2:1 is a
MuJoCo equality constraint rather than an animation - verified at ratio 2.00.

`cad.hardware.horn_joints()` is the check that came out of it: **8 of 10 horn
joints seat at 0.00 mm**, where all ten were failing and two were clashing
1.10 mm. That 1.10 is `HORN_OVERALL_T - SPLINE_PROUD`: parts were positioned at
the spline tip, and the horn finishes 1.10 mm past it. The thigh and shin got
horn pockets; the roll bracket's coupler face is now derived from the horn.

### A parallelogram beats the ankle servo

`docs/deleting-the-ankle-pitch-servo.md`. Foot mode, 15 s, 10 trials:

| lash | servo + ankle loop | parallelogram, no loop |
|---|---|---|
| 2 deg | 2/10 | **10/10** |
| 3 deg | 3/10 | **10/10** |
| 4 deg | 2/10 | 0/10 |

Control-tested: removing the ankle's lash alone is worth nothing (0/5 at 3 deg).
The linkage wins because it is a mechanical feedback path, not because it is
rigid. `load(parallel=True)` builds it as a tendon equality.

**And it found something worse.** `docs/backlash.md` records foot mode standing
at 2-3 deg with the loop on. It does not any more. That table was measured
against the old inertia, and `SEG_INERTIA` was regenerated from the CAD on
08-02. Correcting the mass distribution, which was right, **broke foot mode at
the lash the servos actually have.** The table is marked superseded in place.

## Open, ranked by what it costs at the bench

**1. Draw the parallelogram.** DONE later the same day, see below. It fits.

It is proven as a constraint and not as a
mechanism. It deletes the belt drive (which provably does not fit), 180 g, two
servos, the 4.40 mm pulley hub that is not a catalogue part, and the outboard
bearing that makes this robot 306 mm wide. Kinematics do not change:
`hip + knee + ankle = 0` is what is commanded today.

The corridor is found and there is only one. A 5x5 mm link swept knee to ankle
against the real obstacles:

```
x = 34, y = 36     12 mm3    essentially clear
x = 24, y = 36    432 mm3
everywhere else   774 - 2243 mm3
```

Aft and outboard, which is where the belt currently runs. Note the topology:
holding the foot parallel to the TORSO needs two parallelograms in series, one
beside the thigh and one beside the shin, not a single link.

> **The table above was never committed as code and it does not reproduce.**
> Re-swept from `cad/linkage.py`, the corridor is much wider than one cell and
> the answer is a different one: the linkage goes OUTBOARD at an offset of
> 30 mm, and inboard - which is where "y = 36" points - is where the yoke
> cannot be reached. The topology note was right.

**2. The hip and roll servos have no seating face.** Captured with 0.4 mm
clearance on every face, which is **1.85 deg of free rotation** at the hip, in a
10.1 deg tilt budget that already spends 2-3 deg on gear lash. Three of five
servos seat at 0.000; these two seat on nothing. Probably worth more to foot
mode than any control work.

**3. The wheel stands 4.40 mm off its horn.** `cad/wheel.py` documents the
intent - pocket floor on the plate, rim 0.5 mm clear of the boss face - and the
assembly does not match it. Closing it moves the wheel (track width, stance,
flip envelope) or the servo (kinematics), so it needs a decision.

**4. The belt drive is 2.23 mm underground in foot mode.** The axle sits at
`WHEEL_HALF_W` = 12.0 mm with the wheel flat and the 40T pitch radius is 12.73,
so it does not fit even with no flange. Item 1 deletes this rather than fixing
it; if item 1 is rejected, this becomes blocking and the only escape is a ratio
under 2:1.

**Sequencing.** Do 1 before 2 and 3. The shin and ankle get redrawn by the
linkage, and fixing their seating faces first means fixing them twice. Item 2's
hip half is on the chassis and is independent.

## Smaller things left deliberately

- The mesh build carries 2 more DOF than the plain one - the belt pulley bodies
  only exist when `meshes=True`. They are stub-mass and hard-constrained, so
  dynamically inert, but a twin whose DOF count depends on a display flag is the
  kind of split this project keeps getting bitten by.
- The ankle-pitch horn is not in the sim. It belongs on the `beltdrive` body,
  which only exists in the mesh build, so the export skips it.
- `solid()` still returns the wrong-variant STEP, oversized in z, so clearance
  checks stay conservative. Safe direction.
- Backlash is still unmeasured on real hardware and is still the number this
  design sits closest to. It needs 12 V and a TTL adapter, and there is no
  serial code in this repo.

# Later the same day: the parallelogram is drawn, and it fits

Item 1 above is done. `cad/linkage.py`, `tests/test_linkage.py`, and a section
appended to `docs/deleting-the-ankle-pitch-servo.md`.

**The headline: nothing collides, at any pose, on the real solids, over squat
and flip, both legs.** 32.3 g per leg against 89 g of ankle servo and belt
drive, so 113 g comes off the robot. Five pivots, three new printed parts, and
two features on parts that already exist.

## What turned out to be wrong

**The published corridor does not reproduce, and the code that found it was
never committed.** The table above says one cell is clear (x = 34, y = 36) and
everything else is 774 to 2243 mm3. Re-swept against the same solids, the
clear region is a band tens of millimetres wide, and it is on the other side of
the leg: the linkage goes OUTBOARD. That is not a small correction. Inboard
there is a 59 mm empty corridor between the legs and the rods fit in it
beautifully - and the ankle yoke cannot be reached from there at all, because
it has no material inboard of y = -6, the roll bracket sweeps y = -23..+24
through the flip, and the one straight arm that clears the bracket ends up 1 mm
off the floor in foot mode. Outboard, stage 2's arm is 30 mm of an existing
yoke member carried further forward.

**`rollout_deploy(parallel=True)` returned 0 of 10, not 10 of 10.** This was
the first thing tried, on the assumption that the flag reproduced the published
table, and it came back WORSE than the servo it replaces at both 2 and 3
degrees. The flag built the linkage and left the ankle ACTUATOR in, so the
STAND loop drove the ankle to one angle while the tendon held it at another.
The published 10/10 is real; reproducing it needed the caller to also switch
the ankle loop off, and nothing said so because the measurement was never
committed. `load(parallel=True)` now removes the ankle actuators, which is what
a parallelogram physically is, and the default config gives 10/10.

**`cad/belt.py`'s `JOINT_PEAK_NM = 1.68` is stale.** The measured design ankle
torque is 1.082 N.m. That literal predates `cad/loads.py` being able to produce
the number, and `loads.py` could not produce it either: `survey()` ranks a
whole wrench and reports the instant that peaks, which is not the instant the
joint's own axis peaks. `loads.pitch_survey()` and `design_pitch_torque()` are
new and are what a drive should be sized from. The belt is being deleted, so
the stale number is recorded rather than fixed.

## The number that was not on anyone's list

The linkage is bought to get rid of 2-3 degrees of ankle gear lash. Drawing it
splits that into three quantities that do not behave alike:

| | worst case | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| free play, printed 0.10 mm bores | **0.97 deg** | the same animal as gear lash |
| free play, a 623ZZ at each pin | **0.10 deg** | ditto, for $1.60 a leg |
| build offset, print +/-0.3 mm | **4.21 deg** | a fixed tilt, per leg |
| band spread after adjustment | **1.09 deg** | what no adjustment removes |

It wins the argument it was having, by 2x on plain bores and 20x on bearings.

**And it introduces a fault the servo version did not have.** 4.2 degrees of
fixed foot tilt is not lash - it is a part that comes out of the machine aimed
slightly wrong - and with no ankle actuator there is nothing left to trim it
with. In foot mode that is the torso standing 4 degrees off vertical with both
soles flat, and the two legs are free to be wrong in opposite directions.

> **One of the two rods has to be length-adjustable.** That is a requirement
> this design did not have this morning, and it exists because the thing got
> drawn. `tests/test_linkage.py` asserts the size of it so that shrinking it
> counts and forgetting it does not.

## Also worth knowing

- **Transmission angle** holds at sin >= 0.931 over the band. Which of the two
  idler pins goes high is not a free choice: stage 1 low and stage 2 high keeps
  the rods from crossing AND keeps stage 2's pin above the axle, which is 12 mm
  off the floor in foot mode. The other way round crossed the rods for 950 mm3
  and put the yoke arm on the ground.
- **Rod force 38.7 N against 705 N of buckling.** The rods are not the problem.
- **The chassis fin is the worst part of the design.** 79 mm off the side plate
  to hold stage 1's ground pin, and a pin that moves is a foot that tilts at
  2 deg/mm. As a round boss that is 0.7 mm and 1.3 degrees - more than the free
  play the mechanism is bought for. It is a 25 mm fin instead, at 0.1 mm, and
  it wants triangulating back to the plate.
- **The clearance check found four faults in its own subject** before it found
  any in the robot: four pairs of linkage parts sharing a y band by 430 to
  950 mm3, every one of them a rod's eye inside the thing it is pinned to. The
  parts now live in a five-slice stack across y. Two of the slices are set by
  the wheel and the wheel-drive servo, and two by the shin's ankle carrier.
- **And the clearance check had a hole of its own**, which is the day's best
  reminder that a new check is the least trustworthy thing in the repo. It
  built its pair list as one product with `if na >= nb: continue` to drop the
  duplicate self-pairs, and that name comparison silently threw away every pair
  whose partner sorted before `lk_`. Exactly one part does: `ankle`, the yoke,
  which is where stage 2 lives. It read as 13 poses of green for an hour. Fixed,
  still clean, and control-tested by moving stage 2 inboard into the flat wheel,
  which brings back 10 colliding pairs.

## Still open, and it is the same list plus one

The ranked list above stands, with item 1 struck out and this added:

**Fold the linkage into the parts it belongs to.** Three of its six pieces are
features of parts that already exist - the fin is chassis, the stub is shin, the
arm is yoke - and they are drawn in `cad/linkage.py` rather than cut into
`cad/chassis.py`, `cad/shin.py` and `cad/ankle.py`. Until that happens the
linkage is checked but not built, and `cad.stress`, `cad.twin`, `cad.fitview`
and the mass budget know nothing about it. That is also when the belt, the
pulleys, the ankle servos and the shin's outboard bearing carrier come out, and
when the robot stops being 306 mm wide.

**Nothing else moved.** `drives`, `twin`, `fasteners`, `wiring`,
`assemble_check` (all four), `fitcheck` and `printability` are all still clean,
and `printability` now covers the five new parts.

# Later still: the linkage is the robot now, not a proposal

Items 1 to 4 of the ranked list above are done, and the order sheet with them.
**1907 g to 1800 g, ten servos to eight, and every check green.**

## What turned out to be wrong

**The 10/10 foot-mode result was measured on a robot still carrying the servo
it had deleted.** The parallelogram lived in `cad/` and nowhere else: the sim
still had the ankle servo, the belt, and their 89 g a leg. So `cad.twin` -
whose entire job is catching sim-versus-CAD drift - was blind to the largest
divergence in the repo, and the headline number was measured against mass that
was supposed to be gone.

Taking it out properly made it better, not worse. Foot mode now stands **10/10
at four degrees of gear lash**, where the same linkage on the old mass failed
four ten times out of ten. The cliff moved from between 3 and 4 degrees to
between 4 and 5, against servos that have 2-3. Deleting 89 g from the far end
of the leg bought a degree of backlash margin, and no tendon in a constraint
could have told you that.

**The wheel's 4.40 mm standoff was written up as a decision about the robot's
proportions and it is a change to one part.** "Move the wheel and change the
track, or move the servo and change the kinematics." Neither. The horn's plate
sits entirely on the SERVO side of the seating face, so what was missing was
hub, and hub grows outboard into space the servo already occupies. The tyre
does not move; the track, the stance and the flip envelope are untouched. 8 of
8 horn joints seat now.

**The hip and roll servos do not "seat on nothing".** Measure the gap from
every servo to the part holding it and all eight come back 0.000 mm. They all
touch. Touching is not being located, which is the actual fault, and the
published 1.85 degrees had no code behind it. `cad.fasteners.capture()` rotates
each case about its own shaft until something stops it: **hip and roll 1.30
degrees, knee 0.70, wheel 0.10.**

**`cad/belt.py`'s JOINT_PEAK_NM = 1.68 was stale** - the measured design ankle
torque is 1.082 - and `cad/loads.py` could not produce that number either,
because `survey()` ranks a whole wrench and the instant a wrench peaks is not
the instant a joint's own axis peaks. `pitch_survey()` and
`design_pitch_torque()` are new.

**CRADLE_CLEAR was in four files.** They all said 0.4, which is what a second
copy looks like right up until it is not.

## Two checks that were wrong rather than merely stale

`tests/test_stage0`'s mass bound was a second copy of a number in
`cad/masses.py`. It asks the CAD now, so it cannot go stale on its own.

And a cliff test I wrote this morning ran ten trials that were the same trial
ten times: it varied the DURATION, which does not change the initial condition,
and passed at a lash the robot demonstrably cannot flip at. Fixing it produced
a finding worth more than the test: **standing in foot mode is not what lash
breaks, arriving in it is.** Dropped straight in, already flat, the robot
stands at 6 degrees. It cannot flip there.

## The number that decided the adjuster

The worst-case build offset is 4.21 degrees of fixed foot tilt and 4.21 on its
own says nothing. So it was measured, by injecting the offset into the tendon
equality - which is exactly what a mis-built linkage does, still rigid, just
holding the wrong number - and flipping at 3 degrees of lash:

| offset | both legs the same way | opposed |
|---|---|---|
| **4.21 deg** | 8/8 | **7/8** |
| 5.0 deg | 8/8 | 4/8 |
| 5.5 deg | 0/8 | 1/8 |

The worst case does not sit near the cliff, it is **already over** stage 2's
exit criterion of zero falls - and over in the direction two separately printed
legs actually go, which is opposed rather than together. Rod 2 is two clamped
halves now, +/- 3 mm, worth 6.08 degrees against the 4.21 it has to cover.

## What the servo mounts cost, priced rather than deplored

Capture slop is in series with the gearbox, so it adds to that joint's gear
lash:

```
3.0 deg gear lash alone            8/8
4.3 deg, with the worst capture    8/8
5.5 deg                            0/8
```

It does not break the robot, it spends the margin: 3 degrees of lash on its own
sits 1.5 degrees from the cliff and the worst capture is 1.3 of that. Not
fixed, deliberately - the clearance is 0.4 mm because the service quotes +/-0.3
and a tighter cradle risks not assembling, so the answer is a compliant preload
on one wall, and two of the four parts that needs are about to be redrawn.

## Still open, ranked

**1. Redraw the shin, and fold the three linkage features into their hosts.**
`lk_fin` is chassis, `lk_stub` is shin, `lk_arm` is ankle yoke. Until then the
order sheet describes a robot that cannot be assembled, and it says so.

The shin is the part with real work in it, and it is measured now rather than
guessed at. **It still carries the ankle-pitch servo's mount: STANDOFF is
9.40 cm3 of its 53.05, about 7 g a leg, plus two cradle walls** - a mount for a
servo that is not there. And its forward carrier (FARM, FPOST, CROSS, DROP,
BACK) only reaches y = 136 because the deleted BELT forced the ankle bearing
out there. Both come out together or the packaging gets re-checked twice.
`cad/envelope.py`'s bands are already wider than that file last reported.

Meanwhile the dead mount is real in the model, so every clearance and mass
number above is conservative rather than optimistic.

**2. The servo capture preload.** 1.3 degrees, priced above, on four parts.

**3. The printed mass is quoted at PETG's 1.27 g/cm3 and these parts are
ordered in PA6-CF at 1.19.** About 26 g the model carries that the robot will
not. Not fixed because the material is not settled - three PA12 variants came
back into play when the roll bracket went from 99% to 55%.

**4. Backlash is still unmeasured on real hardware**, and everything above is
quoted against it. It needs 12 V and a TTL adapter, and there is no serial code
in this repo.

**5. `cad.stress` has never solved a linkage part.** The rods are 18x from
buckling by hand calculation and no FEA has seen them.
