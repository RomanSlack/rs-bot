## The horn, and how it was finally measured

The horn is a bought part, so it is not in the servo STEP - that file holds one
solid, the bare case. The pattern had therefore always been assumed.

It did not need hardware. **A part that MATES with the horn carries the horn's
pattern as its own clearance holes**, and TheRobotStudio publish theirs. Six
patterns across three SO-ARM101 parts, agreeing on position to 0.00 mm:

    Rotation_Pitch_SO101    r = 1.60   +/-4.95 x +/-4.95
    Wrist_Roll_Pitch_SO101  r = 1.60   +/-4.95 x +/-4.95
    Upper_arm_SO101         r = 1.50   +/-4.95 x +/-4.95

Position was nearly right. Size was not: r = 1.25 -> 1.60, which is the
difference between M2.5 and M3. A 3.2 mm clearance hole in plastic also means
the thread is in the metal horn, so horn joints need no heat-set inserts - the
fastener count went from 48 inserts to 16, plus 32 M3.

`vendor/refs/Rotation_Pitch_SO101.step` is vendored so this is re-derivable.

**There is a decoy and it is a good one.** The case carries four r = 1.25 screws
at EACH end on a 9.9 x 9.9 rectangle - almost exactly what this project believed
the horn to be - centred on x = 11.25 rather than the shaft at 12.50. Anyone
re-deriving the pattern will find them and think they have found the horn.

## What else the buildability work found

**A 0.1 mm running clearance.** The roll bracket's arm ran parallel to the whole
face of a wheel that turns at 40 rad/s. That is a drawing clearance, not a
manufacturing one: the services quote +/-0.3 mm, so it is inside the tolerance
band of both parts. Now 0.9 mm.

**A servo standing off its own mount.** The wheel-drive servo sat 0.5 mm from the
bracket it bolts to, from someone clearing a 0.05 mm interference by moving
0.55. On a standoff the reaction couple goes into the bolts in BENDING with no
friction preload. Now seats flat.

Neither was visible before, because `assemble_check` only tested for
interference and 0.5 mm of air reads exactly like 20 mm of air. It now reports
minimum distance and distinguishes a running clearance from a bolted face that
is supposed to touch.

**Cables, which did not exist at all.** Ten daisy-chained servos, a lead across
every joint, one of which turns 90 degrees. Now modelled from the measured
connector, with channels cut in the thigh, shin and yoke for BOTH poses.

**The leg solved as one solid.** 13.4 mm at the foot against 14.45 summed from
the parts alone - they agree to 8%, which means the per-part method has not been
lying.

## And the chassis got closed

Open front and back is a U-section, the worst shape there is in torsion, and
docs/cad.md had wanted a rear brace for a while:

    deflection   4.94 -> 0.69 mm     7x stiffer
    utilisation  18/18 -> 14/5 %
    cost         17 g

The panels start at z = 45.4 because the Pi has 2.5 mm at each end, and the
front is a frame because the battery could not otherwise come out.

## Traps, for whoever is next

Every one of these produced a confident, wrong, fully-formatted answer.

**The sim does not store servo boxes in cad/servo.py's axis order.** It writes
the three dimensions in whatever sequence puts the case where it goes, so the
shaft is local y for four servos and local x for the roll. Assume local z and
every screw in the robot reads as perpendicular to its servo - a complete false
alarm. Derive the shaft by matching the box side against HEIGHT.

**A fillet and a bolt hole are both cylindrical faces** and nothing about the
radius separates them. Filleting the shin at r = 1.5 put six new "M2-scale
holes" into the fastener audit. What separates them is angular extent: a hole is
a full 2*pi barrel, a blend is a quarter of one.

**A fillet is not free in the analysis.** Filleting the shin and yoke gave the
curvature mesher more to chase and took the leg assembly from 38k nodes to 62.6k
- past the solver's memory ceiling.

**A mesh too coarse for its part invents stress.** The thigh reported 143% of
PA6-CF and a failure; it is at 30%. Refining collapsed the peak 135 -> 17.7 MPa
while deflection did not move at all, which is the signature. Its default is now
2.2 mm.

**The FEA can exhaust the machine.** A direct sparse factorisation's memory is
not linear in the mesh: 65k nodes reached 32 GB here and the kernel OOM-killer
took an unrelated application with it. `cad/fea.MAX_NODES` refuses above 55k.
Do not raise it casually.

## What is left before ordering

1. **Buy ONE servo and ONE horn, about $30.** It settles the servo case mounting
   positions, the central horn screw, the printed bore tolerance and the 0.35 mm
   tyre press fit. Four unknowns that no simulation produces.
2. **Route the roll-to-wheel cable.** The only run still blocked. It crosses the
   roll joint and its shortest path goes through the WHEEL in foot mode - you
   cannot channel a part that spins. It needs a clip and a tie point, and a tie
   point is a printed feature, so it must exist before anything is printed.
3. **Decide servo capture.** Justified on merit - a rim takes the reaction in
   bearing across the case face where four M2 self-tappers take 136 N each - and
   measured as feasible, 3-4 mm free at every mount. But it is a four-part
   redesign, not a feature: every servo mount here is a plate on the case's end
   face with no material around the perimeter, so a rim has nothing to grow
   from. One $15 servo also tells you whether it is needed at all.

## Suggested next step

**Stop simulating and spend the $30.**

Today found real faults, and it also found three of its own tooling being wrong,
each needing a control test to catch. That ratio is the signal: the simulation
has largely stopped telling us about the robot and started telling us about the
simulation. Everything left in CAD is downstream of a measurement that needs a
part in hand.

## The rule, unchanged

> A check that cannot fail is worse than no check at all, because it reads as
> evidence.

Every fault above was found by a check made able to fail, and three false alarms
were killed by control tests rather than by the checks themselves. Before
trusting anything added here, break what it tests on purpose and confirm it
screams.
